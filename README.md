# eye-kea-exploration

Data and analysis code for:

> Wan, Q., Moriarty, N., Duarte, S. E., Ghetti, S., & Geng, J. J. *The development of navigation and attentional abilities shapes how children explore naturalistic environments.* (Manuscript under revision; the title may change.)

Children (7–14 years) and adults explored a virtual furniture store in VR over two blocks. Multisensory objects (animations with sound) were triggered as participants came near them. Participants then searched the store for nine target objects. Navigation was by teleporting.

The repository holds the de-identified data derived from the recordings and the code that produces every statistic in the paper from those data. The code that turns raw recordings into these data is included as well. The raw recordings themselves are not shared.

## Reproducing the results

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt          # Python 3.8+
Rscript -e 'install.packages(c("lme4", "lmerTest", "emmeans", "pbkrtest", "jsonlite"))'
bash analysis/run_all.sh
```

Tested with Python 3.8.10 (pandas 2.0.3, numpy 1.24.4, scipy 1.10.1, statsmodels 0.14.1, matplotlib 3.7.5) and R 4.4.2 (lme4 1.1-36, lmerTest 3.1-3, emmeans 1.10.7, pbkrtest 0.5-3).

The statistics reported in the manuscript come from `participant_stats.*`, `model_comparison_common_sample/`, `event_models.*`, `hesitation.txt` and `figure5_means.csv`. `encounters_backtracking.txt` and the pooled correlations in `participant_stats.txt` are computed for completeness and labeled as not reported.

`results/sample_description.txt` (ages and gender as group totals) is written by the processing step, because it needs records that are not shared.

## Analysis decisions

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Participants | 42 adults; 41 children who completed both exploration blocks and the search task (49 tested; 7 lost to equipment malfunction, 1 with incomplete data) |
| Outliers | A participant is excluded if Cook's D > 4/n **and** \|studentized residual\| > 2.5. For the models relating backtracking and adaptation to an outcome, the rule is applied once per outcome, in the full model, and every model for that outcome is fit to the remaining participants (`common_sample` in `common.py`). Age regressions apply the rule to each model separately. At most two participants are excluded from any analysis, and results without exclusions are reported alongside (Table S5) |
| Encounters with a multisensory object | Counted across both blocks. The first four encounters are analyzed: 61 of 83 participants reached a fourth encounter with at least one object, 37 a fifth. Later encounters are kept in `data/events.csv` but not analyzed |
| Adaptation | Per participant, the slope of the change in stopping time (5 s after minus 5 s before an event) across encounters with multisensory objects |
| Joint models | Backtracking and adaptation centered at the mean of the common sample. The intercept-only model and the 18 combinations of backtracking, adaptation, age group and their interactions are compared. The selected model is favored by both AIC and BIC; when they disagree, it is better by more than 2 on one criterion and within 2 on the other (`06_model_comparison_common_sample.py`) |
| Mixed models | Random intercepts for participants; Kenward–Roger degrees of freedom; Tukey-adjusted post-hoc contrasts |

## Measures

- **Spatial coverage.** The proportion of 100 grid cells (10 × 10 over the store; 6.23 × 6.84 m each) entered across both blocks.
- **Objects looked at.** Objects (not floors, walls, ceilings, doors or windows) with a gaze fixation event. The experiment software logs this event after about 100 ms of gaze on an object.
- **Object coverage.** Of the catalogued furniture and decor objects in the grid cells a participant entered, the share they looked at. This separates looking at objects from how far a participant traveled.
- **Backtracking.** Head positions are resampled to waypoints 5 m apart. An episode is a turn of at least 120° (equivalently, an angle of 60° or less between the outbound and return paths, as the manuscript describes it) followed by a return path that runs alongside the outbound path. The criteria are:
  - within 8 m of the outbound path for at least 80% of points, in both directions;
  - at least 4 waypoints on each side of the turn;
  - each phase 30–70% of the episode;
  - episode area at most 400 m².

  Backtracking duration and distance are those of the return phase, summed over blocks.
- **Search.** Nine trials, one per target (the latest recording if a trial was restarted). A trial is a success if the object finally selected is the target; a time-out counts as a failure. Search time is the mean over successful trials.

## Data not shared

Raw head- and gaze-tracking recordings, interaction logs, questionnaires and demographic records are not shared. They contain information that could identify participants. Participant codes in `data/` (A01–A42 for adults, C01–C41 for children) were assigned in random order. The key is kept by the authors.
